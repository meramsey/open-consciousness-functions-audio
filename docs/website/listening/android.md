# Android audio settings

**Verified against linked vendor documentation: 2026-11-11.** Menus differ by
manufacturer, Android version, headphones, and media app. These steps are a
checklist, not a guarantee that all device processing is disabled. OCF cannot
detect your device's audio effects.

## Baseline

- Stereo headphones or stereo earbuds with separate left/right playback are
  required for the intended binaural/channel-separated audio. Speakers, mono
  mixing, or a single earpiece are not equivalent delivery of that audio. If you
  cannot use this setup, use the written guide or an edition-matched transcript
  if available, or skip the audio; no equivalent effects are promised.
- Wired headphones are one option; compatible Bluetooth stereo headphones can
  also provide separate left/right playback.
- Keep left/right balance centered unless you use an accessibility adjustment.
- Start at a comfortable, clearly audible level. Keep normal playback speed
  (1.0x); turn off unwanted repeat, shuffle, crossfade, and optional EQ/effects.
- Do not raise the volume to chase a perceived effect. Audio settings do not
  guarantee an experience.

## Google Pixel

On supported devices, open **Settings → Sound & vibration → Spatial audio** and
turn Spatial Audio off for wired headphones or for the connected wireless
device. With Pixel Buds Pro, also check **Settings → Connected devices → Pixel
Buds Pro → Settings → Spatial Audio** and turn **Head tracking** off when that
option is available. Device, accessory, and media support vary; follow the
current [Google Pixel Spatial Audio help](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/12967594?hl=en).

## Samsung and other Android devices

There is no universal Android menu. Check your connected output and the
manufacturer's sound controls for optional Dolby Atmos, 360 Audio, spatial
audio, surround virtualization, equalizer, or sound-effect processing. Turn
optional effects off when you want the unmodified stereo presentation, if the
device provides that choice. Use the current device manufacturer's support
instructions rather than assuming Pixel menu paths apply.

## Mono audio and accessibility

Accessibility features may offer mono audio and channel balance. Mono mixes the
left/right channels and is not equivalent delivery of the intended
binaural/channel-separated audio. Keep mono or other accessibility settings
enabled if you rely on them. Use the written guide or an edition-matched
transcript if available, or skip the audio; no equivalent effects are promised.

## Troubleshooting

If sound is missing, distorted, unexpectedly quiet, or appears to be mono, check
that the selected output is the intended headphones, inspect the device's left
and right balance, and check the player and manufacturer audio effects. Do not
increase volume beyond a comfortable level.
