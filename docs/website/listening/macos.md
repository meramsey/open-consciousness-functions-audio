# macOS audio settings

**Verified against linked Apple documentation: 2026-11-11.** Controls vary by
macOS version, AirPods model, and app. The site cannot detect sound processing
on your Mac or headphones.

## Baseline

- Use comfortable stereo headphones or earbuds when the selected edition is
  designed for separate left and right channels. Wired headphones are a simple
  option; compatible Bluetooth stereo headphones can also work.
- Select the intended output and keep balance centered unless you use an
  accessibility adjustment.
- Start at a comfortable level and use normal playback speed (1.0x). Turn off
  unwanted repeat, shuffle, crossfade, EQ, and effects where available.

## AirPods Spatial Audio

With supported AirPods and apps, open Control Center, choose **Sound**, select
your AirPods, and choose **Spatial Audio → Off**. Apple states that options can
be per app and hardware-dependent. See [Control Spatial Audio and head tracking
on AirPods](https://support.apple.com/guide/airpods/control-spatial-audio-and-head-tracking-dev00eb7e0a3/web).

## Output, balance, and accessibility

Use **System Settings → Sound → Output** to select headphones and inspect
balance. See Apple's current guide to [change sound output settings on
Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/change-the-sound-output-settings-mchlp2256/27/mac/27).
Review **System Settings → Accessibility → Audio** for mono playback and
headphone accommodations. Keep accessibility settings you need; the stereo
baseline is optional, not a requirement for using the text or audio practice.

## Troubleshooting

If a channel is missing or processing sounds unexpected, reselect the output,
check balance, and inspect the current player and AirPods settings. Do not raise
volume to chase a perceived effect.
